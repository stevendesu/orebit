Optimization idea:
==================
Along with our current depth nibbles ("same" up, "air" down) we could add a second byte of "same +X" and "same +Z". Used for faster cuboid computation
Full reverse Dijkstra may be overkill... start with forward-A*, then just walk the skeleton backwards taking lowest-cost connection. Then expand orthogonally to the 8-ring around each cell

Faster cuboids + faster Dijkstra = faster flat cost on all searches (currently ~600us, of which only ~6us is forward-A*. Could target 60us or so ideally)
Need to profile our flat cost to see where else we spend our time. My understanding is it's mostly reverse-Dijkstra + cuboids

Flood fill is faster than A*... can use it to prove no cheap path exists to a goal (flood "cheap" instead of "identical")

At the block level, perhaps a quadratic distance term when you're outside of the skeleton to discourage looking in the wrong direction

At the block level, perhaps a guard at sea level (if the goal is Y > 63, punish exploring Y < 62) to avoid pathological floods into the ocean
